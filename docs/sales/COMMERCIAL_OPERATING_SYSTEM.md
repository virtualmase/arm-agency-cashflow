# ARM Agency Commercial Operating System

**Purpose:** Turn an inquiry into one of three clear outcomes: a qualified bounded engagement, a clearly scoped strategic proposal, or a respectful no/nurture decision.  
**Boundary:** This is a commercial operating template. Any contractual language must receive appropriate legal review before use.

## 1. Sales Operating Principles

The sales system exists to protect client outcomes and delivery capacity. Its purpose is not to force a close. ARM Agency should sell only when the buyer’s problem, decision authority, constraints, and delivery fit are sufficiently clear to support a defined commitment.

> **Rule:** If the team cannot write a truthful scope, the appropriate next step is discovery, a bounded diagnostic, or a no—not an unpriced custom promise.

## 2. Qualification Scorecard

Score each dimension from **0** to **2**. A score guides the conversation; it does not replace professional judgment.

| Dimension | 0 — no evidence | 1 — partial evidence | 2 — clear evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem clarity | Buyer describes a vague interest in AI or visibility. | A workflow or visibility problem is identified but not bounded. | Specific decision, workflow, risk, or operating need is defined. |
| Decision authority | No sponsor or authority path is visible. | A contact has influence but cannot confirm next steps. | Sponsor or authorized delegate is engaged. |
| Urgency and timing | No real timing driver or review date. | General interest within a future period. | A decision, project, or operating constraint has a specific timeline. |
| Delivery fit | Request is outside current scope or requires unsupported claims. | Some fit exists but important constraints are unknown. | Request maps to a bounded offer or a defensible scoped engagement. |
| Access and participation | Needed owners or materials are unavailable. | Some access is available, but key dependencies are uncertain. | Required owners, evidence, and access path can be confirmed. |
| Commercial readiness | Buyer will not discuss a viable commercial path. | Budget or procurement path is partly known. | Budget range, procurement process, or paid diagnostic path is viable. |
| Risk compatibility | Buyer expects impossible guarantees or unrestricted automation. | Risk expectations need clarification. | Client accepts clear boundaries, accountability, and decision rights. |

| Total score | Sales motion | Required next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 11–14 | Qualified | Book scoped discovery or offer the appropriate bounded engagement. |
| 7–10 | Develop | Send a targeted clarification request or recommend a paid diagnostic. |
| 0–6 | Nurture or decline | Provide an educational resource, explain the boundary, and do not force a proposal. |

## 3. First-Response Standards

Every lead receives a response within one business day. The response acknowledges the stated need, sets the purpose of the next conversation, and makes no unverified outcome promise.

### Qualified-service response

> Thank you for the context. ARM Agency works best when we can connect a specific operating decision to a bounded delivery path. The next step is a short qualification conversation to understand the current workflow, decision owners, constraints, and the outcome you need to decide. If there is a fit, we will recommend either a defined diagnostic, a workshop, or a scoped operating engagement with clear deliverables and boundaries.

### Bounded-offer response

> Based on the need you described, the most direct starting point may be the **[AI Infrastructure Audit / Mandate Chain Design Workshop / Custom MCP Tool]**. Before we confirm it, we will validate the decision it needs to inform, the required participants or materials, and the stated acceptance criteria. That keeps the work focused and gives you a useful output even if no follow-on engagement is required.

## 4. Discovery Call Guide

**Format:** 45 minutes. The call ends with a decision: proceed to a bounded offer, prepare a specific proposal, request clarifying material, nurture, or decline.

| Segment | Prompts | Evidence to capture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context | “What decision or operational change prompted this conversation?” | Business driver, timeline, trigger event. |
| Current state | “How is this workflow handled today? Where does it fail or slow down?” | Workflow, people, systems, failure modes. |
| Accountability | “Who owns the decision, operation, technical boundary, and risk approval?” | Sponsor, operator, technical owner, risk contact. |
| Constraints | “What must remain true for this to be acceptable?” | Data, security, budget, timing, procurement, vendor constraints. |
| Desired next step | “What would make this conversation useful in the next 30–60 days?” | Decision outcome, success boundary, non-goals. |
| Commercial path | “What is the appropriate way to fund and approve an initial bounded step?” | Budget signal, purchase path, approval sequence. |
| Close | “Should we recommend a diagnostic, workshop, technical scope, or a different path?” | Selected next action, owner, due date. |

## 5. Proposal and SOW Structure

The proposal must be a decision document, not a sales brochure. Every section should answer a buyer question or prevent scope ambiguity.

| Section | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Decision context | The buyer’s stated need and the decision this scope supports. |
| Objectives | Specific service objectives, written as delivery objectives rather than outcome guarantees. |
| Scope of work | Tasks, deliverables, cadence, meetings, and named work boundaries. |
| Client responsibilities | Access, inputs, participants, review periods, decision owners, and dependencies. |
| Exclusions | Work that is not included, including certifications, legal advice, unmanaged vendor commitments, or open-ended development. |
| Milestones and acceptance | Deliverable sequence, review window, acceptance condition, and change-control process. |
| Commercial terms | Price, payment timing, tax treatment if applicable, and proposal validity period. |
| Assumptions and risks | Conditions that could alter scope, timing, feasibility, or delivery quality. |
| Governance | Status cadence, escalation path, confidentiality/data-handling assumptions, and main contacts. |
| Next action | Approval, proposed revision, clarification request, or respectful decline. |

## 6. Objection-Handling Library

Objection handling should clarify fit. It must never paper over delivery, legal, security, or outcome uncertainty.

| Buyer statement | Appropriate response |
| --- | --- |
| “Can you guarantee rankings, revenue, uptime, or ROI?” | “We can define and report the service activities, evidence, scope, and operational signals. We do not guarantee external platform behavior, business outcomes, or performance figures that are outside the stated contract.” |
| “Can you start with a broad custom AI transformation?” | “We can explore the need, but we recommend beginning with a bounded diagnostic or workshop so the actual decision rights, constraints, and implementation scope are clear before committing to a larger program.” |
| “We need a proposal before sharing detail.” | “We can provide a preliminary scope range and a statement of what information is required. A final commitment needs enough context to make the scope truthful and workable.” |
| “Can you include a feature while you are in there?” | “We can assess the request against the existing acceptance criteria. If it changes the workflow, access, test plan, or delivery effort, we will document it as a scope change rather than add it silently.” |
| “Why do you need decision owners?” | “Clear ownership protects the implementation and the people using it. It tells us who can approve actions, resolve exceptions, and make the next decision when constraints conflict.” |

## 7. Commercial-to-Delivery Handoff

No delivery work starts from a CRM status alone. The sales owner completes a handoff brief and the delivery owner accepts it before kickoff.

| Handoff field | Must be confirmed before kickoff |
| --- | --- |
| Signed scope and payment status | Correct offer, commercial terms, payment state, and any approved exception. |
| Buyer decision | The decision the engagement is intended to support. |
| Deliverables and acceptance criteria | What will be delivered, reviewed, and accepted. |
| Named roles | Sponsor, operational owner, technical owner, delivery owner, and client-success contact. |
| Inputs and access | Evidence, workspace, data, credentials, and safe access boundary. |
| Timing | Kickoff date, milestone dates, review windows, and client deadlines. |
| Constraints and risks | Known technical, security, legal, procurement, capacity, or stakeholder risks. |
| Communication cadence | Status channel, meeting rhythm, escalation route, and decision-log location. |

## 8. Pipeline Hygiene Rules

The sales owner updates every active lead after meaningful contact. A lead record should include the current status, next action, owner, qualification rationale, and next-contact date. “No activity” is not a valid sales status. Lost opportunities are categorized for learning: no timing, no budget, no authority, poor fit, insufficient proof, competitor, or unknown.

## 9. Operating Metrics

| Measure | Why it matters | Review use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead-to-qualified rate | Tests whether marketing and CTA language attract the intended buyer. | Repair source, message, or qualification path. |
| Qualified-to-proposal rate | Tests discovery quality and scope fit. | Improve discovery, proof, or offer design. |
| Proposal-to-paid rate | Tests commercial clarity and perceived value. | Improve scope, price architecture, risk handling, or buying path. |
| Time to first response | Tests operational reliability. | Rebalance ownership or automate assignment—not relationship quality. |
| Scope-change rate | Tests whether sales and delivery define work accurately. | Tighten assumptions, exclusions, and acceptance criteria. |
| Delivery handoff completeness | Tests revenue-to-delivery integrity. | Block kickoff until the checklist is complete. |
