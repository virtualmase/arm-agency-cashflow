# Customer Service Procedure

## Purpose

Use this procedure for support requests received through the approved inbox, portal, contact route, or owner-assigned task. It protects response reliability, client confidentiality, delivery sustainability, and the boundary between useful assistance and unauthorized account or financial actions.

## Service triage

| Request type | Agent may do | Mandatory escalation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General pre-sale question | Provide approved public information and route to qualification | Any custom commitment, price exception, or outcome request |
| Onboarding or delivery question | Draft a factual status/next-step note from the approved handoff | Missing scope, access issue, delayed milestone, or risk condition |
| Portal/invoice/receipt question | Explain available portal or Stripe receipt pathways without viewing unrelated accounts | Account ownership uncertainty, payment discrepancy, invoice change, or personal-data issue |
| Subscription cancellation | Explain the customer self-service cancellation path and preserve the request context | Cancellation failure, complaint, refund request, or contract question |
| Security, privacy, or access concern | Acknowledge receipt and collect only essential facts | Immediately route to technical/agency owner; do not troubleshoot in a public channel |
| Complaint or dissatisfied customer | Acknowledge, summarize facts neutrally, and provide a human next step | Client-success owner reviews before any substantive commitment |

## Response procedure

First verify the requester, account context, and request classification using only necessary information. Review the applicable scope, portal state, client handoff, or public policy. Draft a concise response that states what is known, the next action, who owns it, and any expected review path. Do not imply that a request is resolved until the responsible system or human confirms it.

The agent may prepare a response but may send only through an authenticated, approved sender and only when the assigned service policy permits it. If outbound email is unavailable, preserve the ticket and notify the named owner instead of fabricating a delivery confirmation.

## Escalation and closure

Escalate unresolved delivery, billing, access, privacy, security, cancellation, and complaint matters with a factual summary, client impact, relevant records, proposed next step, and named owner. Close a request only when the requester has a clear resolution, next step, or transfer of ownership. Record recurring questions and handoff friction as operating-improvement signals; do not treat a high ticket volume as evidence of success.

## Employee-sustainability control

At the weekly review, inspect unassigned tickets, repeat escalations, response-time misses, and delivery interruptions. If service demand creates overload, reduce scope, adjust staffing, improve self-service information, or pause promotion. Do not normalize hidden overtime or ask individuals to compensate for an unsafe operating system.
