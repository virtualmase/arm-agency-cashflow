# Agent Governance and Escalation

## Operating principle

Agents support accountable decisions; they do not replace owners. Every agent has a named purpose, allowed data, permitted tools, human escalation path, output record, and stop condition. Lack of a permission is a denial, not an invitation to infer permission from context.

## Authority matrix

| Activity | Future agent may prepare | Future agent may execute | Required approver |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Offer and campaign planning | Yes | No external publication or send | Growth owner |
| Inbound lead response | Yes, using approved templates | Only after the approved sender and service path are configured | Client-success owner |
| Discovery preparation and qualification notes | Yes | No binding statements, prices, or commitments | Sales owner |
| Content and creative assets | Yes | No publication, ad launch, or public claim change | Growth owner |
| Voice/inbox assistant design | Yes | Internal testing only until the platform, disclosure, retention, transfer, and escalation controls are approved | Agency owner |
| Customer records and service handoffs | Yes, using minimum necessary data | No access, scope, account, cancellation, or invoice change | Client-success owner |
| Analytics and reporting | Yes | Read-only analysis and draft reports only | Operations owner |
| Finance and revenue operations | Reconciliation and exception summaries only | Never charge, refund, transfer, change pricing, tax settings, bank data, or commercial terms | Agency owner |
| Security and privacy | Risk descriptions and change proposals only | Never weaken controls, reveal credentials, or broaden access | Technical owner |

## Minimum record for an agent task

Every task record includes the user/job, permitted systems and data, requested action, approval state, source material, output location, escalation owner, and retention/deletion boundary. Store only the minimum client or prospect information needed for the task.

## Escalation classes

| Class | Examples | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate stop | Credential exposure, suspected fraud, payment request, security incident, personal-data breach, unlawful contact request, or unsafe system action | Stop the workflow, preserve relevant evidence, and alert the technical or agency owner immediately. |
| Owner decision | Publication, outbound outreach, proposed call campaign, paid spend, pricing, contractual term, customer-access change, scope exception, refund, or cancellation | Prepare a concise decision brief; await explicit approval. |
| Specialist review | Legal interpretation, tax treatment, regulated claim, employment issue, material security architecture, or transaction structure | Flag the issue and route it to the owner and appropriate qualified adviser. |
| Routine human handoff | Complex support, unclear buyer fit, emotionally sensitive communication, delivery risk, or customer dissatisfaction | Provide a factual summary, proposed next step, and named owner. |

## Quality and audit procedure

For an external-facing draft, preserve the source facts, claim boundary, recipient/audience, owner approval, asset version, and final channel/action. For operational actions, preserve the request, system used, data accessed, result, exception, and reviewer. Do not create synthetic success records, testimonials, customer reviews, or performance evidence.

## Stop conditions

Pause an agent immediately if it produces an unsupported claim, contacts an unapproved recipient, bypasses an opt-out, attempts a payment or account action, misroutes client data, exceeds its defined access boundary, or creates a delivery-capacity or employee-sustainability risk. Resume only after the relevant owner documents a correction or explicit exception.
