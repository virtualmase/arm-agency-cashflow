# Continuous Customer Health, Retention, Referral, and Team-Sustainability Loop

**Purpose:** Convert ongoing delivery signals into early action, reliable renewals, respectful referrals, and sustainable workloads. This is a review system, not a customer-scoring theater.

## 1. Weekly Service-Quality Review

The client-success owner and delivery owner review each active engagement for no more than the time needed to make clear decisions. Every amber or red signal gets an owner and review date; no one hides risk behind a general “on track” label.

| Review area | Evidence | Green condition | Amber/Red trigger | Required action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | Kickoff brief, weekly update, change requests | Deliverables and acceptance conditions remain understood | Repeated out-of-scope asks, vague expectations, unapproved change | Clarify, propose scope change, or escalate sponsor decision |
| Progress | Milestones, review windows, dependencies | Next milestone is achievable with named inputs | Missed dependency, no owner response, quality risk | Re-sequence, unblock, or reset timeline/scope |
| Participation | Attendance, decisions, input quality | Appropriate client owners participate | Sponsor/owner unavailable or access delayed | Escalate decision path; do not absorb client inactivity as team overtime |
| Value | Client usage, decision quality, delivery feedback | Client can describe useful output or next decision | Client cannot connect work to stated objective | Reframe, revise plan, or end work respectfully |
| Commercial | Payment and renewal timeline | Current terms and next decision are visible | Payment, renewal, procurement, or cancellation issue | Confirm facts, preserve customer rights, assign owner |
| Team sustainability | Workload, interruptions, satisfaction signal | Delivery fits planned capacity | Excess overtime, handoff failure, repeated escalation, low satisfaction | Reduce scope/load, add support, change ownership, or pause intake |

## 2. Monthly Customer Health Decision

| Health outcome | Meaning | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continue | Scope, progress, client participation, value, and team capacity are adequate | Continue agreed plan and document next decision. |
| Recover | A material but solvable risk exists | Create a short recovery plan with owner, sponsor visibility, and review date. |
| Re-scope | Work remains valuable but the current boundary is wrong | Use the scope-change process; do not silently add work. |
| Renew | Current service remains useful and the client has enough evidence to make a next-term decision | Start renewal discussion early with clear options and constraints. |
| Refer | Client has expressed genuine value and account health is green | Ask permission for a specific, appropriate introduction; accept no without pressure. |
| Close | Work is complete, no longer appropriate, or cannot be delivered safely | Provide handoff, confirm access/billing state, invite factual feedback, and close respectfully. |

## 3. Renewal Review Template

Start a renewal review before the final billing or service-end window, not at the moment a client is forced to decide.

| Question | Evidence required |
| --- | --- |
| What was the agreed delivery objective? | Original scope and accepted milestones |
| What was delivered or learned? | Accepted outputs, decision records, and client feedback |
| What remains unresolved? | Dependencies, risks, client actions, or future scope need |
| What next-term options are genuinely appropriate? | Continue, narrow, expand, pause, or close—each with a real delivery boundary |
| Is the team able to accept the next term sustainably? | Delivery-owner capacity confirmation and employee-health signal |

> **Renewal integrity rule:** Do not propose expansion to solve an unresolved delivery or relationship problem. Stabilize or close the existing scope first.

## 4. Referral Approach

A referral request follows a demonstrated service experience, not a campaign quota. It must not use invented testimonials, ratings, case studies, or implied endorsement.

> “If you know an operator facing a similar decision, would you be comfortable making a permission-based introduction? A useful introduction would include their role, the problem they are trying to solve, and whether a bounded diagnostic, workshop, or implementation conversation would be appropriate. No pressure if there is no fit.”

| Referral quality condition | Why it protects quality |
| --- | --- |
| Client health is green | Prevents asking for advocacy while value is unresolved. |
| Client has used or accepted a meaningful output | Ensures the request follows actual experience. |
| Permission is explicit | Protects the client relationship and recipient privacy. |
| Need and buyer role are contextualized | Prevents indiscriminate outreach. |
| The right first step is stated | Keeps referrals aligned with bounded offers and capacity. |

## 5. Employee-Sustainability Loop

The internal satisfaction module provides a signal, but the response is operational. A declining signal prompts inquiry into workload, interruptions, unclear scope, uneven handoffs, and psychological safety—not a superficial morale intervention.

| Signal | Review question | Possible response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Satisfaction declines | Which work conditions changed? | Rebalance workload, remove friction, improve support, or reduce commitments. |
| Workload rises | Is the demand temporary, planned, and supported? | Re-sequence, revise scope, add capacity, or pause new intake. |
| Repeat escalation | Is there a systemic scope, process, or access failure? | Fix root cause and update playbook, proposal, or technical control. |
| Low feedback participation | Is the channel trusted and usable? | Improve psychological safety, anonymity boundary, and management follow-through. |
