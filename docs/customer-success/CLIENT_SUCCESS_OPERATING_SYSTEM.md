# ARM Agency Client Success and Sustainable Delivery System

**Purpose:** Ensure that every paid engagement starts with a clear handoff, runs with visible ownership and decision cadence, protects delivery quality, and approaches renewal or referral only after contracted value is understood.  
**Operating principle:** A healthy client relationship and a sustainable team are two sides of the same delivery system.

## 1. First 30 Days: Client Onboarding Standard

The first 30 days should reduce uncertainty for both the client and the delivery team. The goal is not to impress the client with activity; it is to establish a shared operating model, confirm the delivery boundary, and achieve the first meaningful milestone.

| Timing | Client experience | Internal control | Required evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Payment confirmation | Receipt, thank-you route, and a clear statement of the next contact | Reconcile payment, contract/scope, and portal access before fulfillment begins | Stripe/webhook record and commercial handoff brief agree. |
| Within one business day | Welcome note with named client-success owner and next action | Confirm delivery owner, sponsor, technical owner, and operational owner | Contacts and responsibilities are recorded. |
| Before kickoff | Clear agenda, required inputs, access request, and success boundary | Delivery owner accepts the handoff and capacity commitment | Kickoff brief and access checklist are complete. |
| Kickoff | Scope, assumptions, milestones, decisions, risks, and communication cadence are reviewed | Record decisions and unresolved dependencies | Shared action plan with named owners. |
| Week 1 | First work output or validated current-state view | Check scope risk, access readiness, and team workload | Status note includes achievements, risks, decisions, and next milestone. |
| Weeks 2–4 | Visible progress against the agreed plan | Review health, value evidence, and any scope change | Client health record and updated action plan. |

## 2. Client Welcome Template

> Subject: Welcome to ARM Agency — Your first milestone
>
> Thank you for choosing ARM Agency. Your primary point of contact is **[client-success owner]** and your delivery owner is **[delivery owner]**. Our first objective is to confirm the scope, decision owners, required inputs, and the first milestone described in your engagement.
>
> Before kickoff, please confirm the listed sponsor, operational owner, technical owner, and any security or procurement contact. We will use the kickoff to validate assumptions, record open questions, agree the status cadence, and identify the conditions that require escalation. If any requested work falls outside the agreed scope, we will document it and discuss an appropriate next step rather than add it silently.

## 3. Kickoff Agenda and Decision Record

| Segment | Client outcome | Internal outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope and decision | Client confirms what this engagement is intended to decide or deliver | Delivery owner confirms that scope can be accepted safely. |
| Roles and decision rights | Client knows who approves, supplies inputs, and escalates | Team knows who can resolve blockers. |

## 4. Weekly Delivery Update

Every active engagement receives a concise weekly update. The update does not disguise blockers, overstate progress, or create unnecessary meeting load. It should be useful to a sponsor and actionable to the operating team.

| Section | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Completed this period | Deliverables, decisions, tests, or client inputs completed against the agreed plan. |
| Next period | The next small set of actions and the owner for each. |
| Decisions needed | Specific client decision, information, approval, or access requirement. |
| Risks and dependencies | What could change delivery timing, quality, scope, or safety; no vague “all good” reporting. |
| Scope change | Any request that changes deliverables, interface, access, schedule, or acceptance condition. |
| Health signal | Green/amber/red assessment with a short evidence-based rationale. |

## 5. Customer Health Review

Customer health is not a sentiment score. It is a structured review of whether the contracted operating value is being delivered and whether the relationship is at risk.

| Health area | Green | Amber | Red |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope clarity | Scope and acceptance conditions remain understood. | New requests or assumptions need clarification. | Material disagreement or unbounded expectation exists. |
| Delivery progress | Milestones are moving with expected dependencies. | A dependency or review delay needs attention. | Critical milestone is blocked with no owner response. |
| Client participation | Decision owners attend and supply agreed inputs. | Some inputs or decisions are late. | Necessary owner/access is repeatedly unavailable. |
| Value evidence | Client can see useful outputs against the engagement purpose. | Value needs clearer framing or review. | The buyer questions value or the agreed purpose is no longer relevant. |
| Commercial health | Payment and renewal expectations are clear. | Procurement, payment, or renewal steps need work. | Material commercial issue threatens continuity. |
| Team sustainability | Workload and support demand fit the delivery plan. | Workload or handoffs need adjustment. | Quality or employee health is at risk; scope/capacity action is required. |

### Required Response

An amber account receives a named recovery action and review date. A red account receives an escalation within one business day and may require a scope reset, capacity change, delivery pause, or sponsor decision. Expansion is never proposed to solve a health problem.

## 6. Renewal and Referral Rules

Renewal should be a decision about continuing a useful service, not a last-minute commercial surprise. Referral requests should happen only after the client can describe value in their own words and has no unresolved service-risk issue.

| Motion | Trigger | Required preparation | Do not proceed when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Renewal review | 30–45 days before an ongoing term ends, or at agreed review point | Summarize delivered scope, current health, open risks, next-term options, and decision deadline | Client health is red or contracted delivery remains unresolved. |
| Expansion discussion | Client’s validated next decision requires additional scope | Propose a new bounded scope with assumptions and acceptance criteria | Expansion is being used to compensate for unclear existing work. |
| Referral request | Client has confirmed value and relationship health is green | Ask permission, provide a simple description of the right introduction, and respect a no | The client is waiting on a critical deliverable or has not expressed value. |
| Cancellation response | Client requests cancellation or signals non-renewal | Confirm service end date, access, outstanding work, records, and respectful feedback path | The team attempts to obstruct self-service cancellation or pressure the client. |

## 7. Employee Sustainability Controls

Employee satisfaction is an operating signal, not a decorative survey. The existing satisfaction module should be reviewed alongside client load, handoff quality, escalation volume, and unplanned support demand.

| Control | Cadence | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capacity review | Weekly | Do not accept work that exceeds named delivery capacity without a scope, timeline, or staffing change. |
| Workload and satisfaction review | Monthly | If satisfaction or workload risk declines, investigate workload drivers and change the operating plan. |
| Escalation review | Weekly | Repeated escalations indicate a process, scope, or client-expectation issue—not an individual endurance issue. |
| Meeting hygiene | Weekly | Remove meetings that do not create a decision, unblock delivery, or protect client communication. |
| Recovery time | Per project milestone | Plan reasonable recovery after intensive workshops, incident work, or high-context-switch periods. |
| Psychological safety | Continuous | Team members may surface quality, safety, scope, or capacity risk without retaliation or pressure to hide it. |

## 8. Client-Success Dashboard Questions

The owner dashboard should be reviewed with these questions, not as an abstract score screen.

| Question | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Which clients need a decision, input, or risk escalation this week? | Delivery updates, health records, and decision log. |
| Which paid engagements are ready for a renewal review? | Commercial timeline plus green health evidence. |
| Which scope changes are recurring? | Proposal and delivery change records. |
| Are portal, invoice, and cancellation interactions creating support demand? | Portal views, invoice requests, cancellations, and support notes. |
| Is delivery load sustainable? | Satisfaction, workload, overtime/interrupt indicators, and escalation pattern. |

## 9. Exit Checklist

When an engagement ends—whether completed, renewed, paused, or cancelled—the client-success owner confirms the final accepted output, remaining access and billing state, handoff material, unresolved issues, record retention boundary, feedback invitation, and next-contact posture. A respectful ending preserves trust and protects future collaboration.
