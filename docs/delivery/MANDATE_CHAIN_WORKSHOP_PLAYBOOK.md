# Mandate Chain Design Workshop — Delivery Playbook

**Commercial reference:** $1,500 fixed-fee facilitated workshop  
**Primary purpose:** Establish explicit decision rights, constraints, escalation paths, and accountable operating boundaries for a defined AI workflow.  
**This is not:** A legal authority delegation, a production implementation, a compliance certification, or an unlimited architecture engagement.

## 1. Client-Facing Scope

The workshop helps a cross-functional group make the operating decisions that must exist before a production AI workflow can be designed or governed responsibly. Participants leave with a decision record, a mandate-chain draft, an unresolved-question list, and a recommended next sequence.

| Included | Excluded unless separately scoped |
| --- | --- |
| One preparation call or written pre-work review | Multi-day facilitation or organization-wide transformation |
| One half-day facilitated workshop | Building agents, integrations, or governance software |
| Decision-rights map and mandate-chain draft | Legal, regulatory, HR, or security certification advice |
| Escalation and exception-flow discussion | Guarantee of deployment approval or business outcome |
| Written decision record and follow-up | Ongoing program management or custom policy drafting |

## 2. Participant and Pre-Work Requirements

The workshop is only useful when decision makers or their delegated owners are present. If key authority is absent, the session should be converted into discovery rather than treated as a completed design decision.

| Required participant | Workshop contribution |
| --- | --- |
| Executive sponsor or accountable delegate | Confirms business objective, risk boundary, and decision authority. |
| Operational owner | Explains real workflow conditions, exceptions, and escalation needs. |
| Technical owner | Explains system constraints, integration boundaries, and observability requirements. |
| Security/privacy or risk contact, when applicable | Defines non-negotiable controls and review path. |

Pre-work asks each participant to provide the workflow objective, current process, decision moments, known risks, existing policy constraints, and unresolved questions. The facilitator consolidates this material into a working draft before the session.

## 3. Workshop Agenda

| Segment | Time | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Objective and decision boundary | 20 minutes | Statement of what the workflow may and may not decide or execute. |
| Workflow and actor map | 40 minutes | Current-state process, roles, systems, and exception points. |
| Mandate-chain design | 60 minutes | Delegation and approval path for each material decision. |
| Constraints and safeguards | 45 minutes | Data, security, operational, and human-oversight boundaries. |
| Escalation and exception handling | 30 minutes | Conditions that stop automation or require review. |
| Success, evidence, and observability | 25 minutes | What will be observed, reviewed, and reported. |
| Action plan and owner commitments | 20 minutes | Named next actions, dependencies, and decision deadlines. |

## 4. Core Workshop Outputs

| Output | Minimum contents | Acceptance condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mandate-chain draft | Actor, permitted action, decision boundary, approver, escalation trigger | Participants can identify who owns each material decision. |
| Constraint register | Constraint, rationale, source, owner, open question | No material constraint is presented as resolved without owner confirmation. |
| Exception map | Failure or uncertainty condition, stop rule, escalation owner, response | The team can explain what happens when automation should not continue. |
| Decision record | Decision, alternatives considered, owner, date, conditions | Sponsor or delegate confirms the decisions captured. |
| Next-step plan | Short action sequence, named owners, dependencies, timing assumption | Each action is within a named owner’s control or explicitly dependent. |

## 5. Facilitation Rules

The facilitator separates facts from assumptions, does not resolve policy questions without the responsible owner, and records disagreement rather than masking it. The workshop is successful when uncertainty is made visible and assigned, not when every issue is forced into a premature answer.

## 6. Completion and Follow-Up

Delivery is complete when the decision record and mandate-chain draft are shared within three business days, factual corrections are captured, and unresolved issues have named owners. A follow-on ARM implementation scope may be proposed only after the client validates the needed work and decision rights.
