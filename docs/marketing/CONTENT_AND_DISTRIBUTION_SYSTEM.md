# ARM Agency Content and Distribution System

**Purpose:** Build authority that helps the right buyer make a decision, strengthens sales conversations, and remains aligned with actual delivery capability.  
**Primary rule:** Content must explain a real decision, service boundary, deliverable, or operational insight. It must not manufacture credibility through unverified customer outcomes, performance figures, reviews, or guarantees.

## 1. Editorial Standard

ARM Agency content should make a complex buying decision easier. It should be specific about the problem, the decision boundary, the work involved, the limits of the recommendation, and the next appropriate step. The goal is useful authority—not publication volume.

| Content must do | Content must not do |
| --- | --- |
| State what ARM can actually scope, build, facilitate, or operate | Claim rankings, revenue, uptime, client counts, or outcomes without current support |
| Distinguish observation, assumption, and recommendation | Present a hypothesis as an observed client result |
| Explain client responsibilities and service boundaries | Promise unspecified “full transformation” or unlimited support |
| Name the appropriate next decision | Use urgency to hide delivery, commercial, or risk uncertainty |
| Link to a scoped offer or helpful resource | Turn every educational topic into an aggressive sales pitch |

## 4. Content Brief Template

Every piece begins with a short brief. The brief is the quality gate that prevents generic or unsupported writing.

| Field | Required answer |
| --- | --- |
| Buyer and role | Who needs this information to make a decision? |
| Decision | What should the reader be able to decide or prepare after reading? |
| Problem boundary | What specific uncertainty, risk, or operating question does the content address? |
| Evidence | What internal deliverable, policy, source, or qualified expert input supports each material claim? |
| Non-claims | Which results, numbers, or guarantees must explicitly not be implied? |
| Structure | The three to five sections that answer the buyer’s questions. |
| Conversion route | The honest next step: read, download, request scope, purchase, or schedule discovery. |
| Distribution | Where the piece will appear and who will review performance. |

## 5. Distribution Operating System

Distribution should be controlled and conversational until the primary domain is restored. The working deployment can support direct sharing, sales follow-up, partner conversations, and internal rehearsal. Paid acquisition, broad SEO promotion, and primary-domain canonical work remain paused.

| Channel | Appropriate use now | Content format | Operating rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Founder/operator posts | Share a specific decision framework or scoped insight | Short point of view linked to a useful resource | One useful insight; no outcome inflation. |
| Email nurture | Follow up after a lead or qualified conversation | Brief contextual note plus relevant resource | Match the resource to the buyer’s stated need. |
| Direct sales follow-up | Help a buyer clarify a next step | Decision guide, scope card, or delivery playbook excerpt | Do not send generic collateral bundles. |
| Partner outreach | Start an informed collaboration conversation | Eligibility, delivery, or referral explainer | Confirm fit and audience before asking for distribution. |
| Controlled demo | Show the working application to a qualified prospect | Diagnostic path, portal, dashboard, and scoped offer | Use the working deployment and explain the domain-launch hold transparently. |

## 6. Editorial and Approval Workflow

| Stage | Owner | Required check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brief | Growth owner | Buyer decision, evidence source, non-claims, and conversion route are explicit. |
| Draft | Subject-matter owner | Service description matches actual delivery playbook and capacity. |
| Evidence review | Delivery or technical owner | Claims, examples, diagrams, and scope boundaries are accurate. |
| Conversion review | Sales owner | CTA maps to an appropriate offer, qualification path, or resource. |
| Publish or share | Growth owner | Metadata, links, attribution, and date are correct. |
| Review | Owner in weekly cadence | Continue, update, repurpose, or retire based on qualified-conversation signal. |

## 7. Content Performance Questions

The content system does not optimize for impressions alone. Each review should answer whether the material helped the right buyer take the right next step.

| Question | Useful signal | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did the right buyer engage? | Qualified replies, discovery references, direct sharing, or relevant CTA activity | Continue or deepen the topic. |
| Did the piece clarify the offer? | Fewer repetitive discovery questions and better-qualified leads | Use it in sales and onboarding. |
| Did the piece create confusion? | Misaligned inquiries, unsupported expectations, or frequent clarification needs | Tighten the headline, scope, and CTA. |
| Did the CTA match intent? | A sensible next action relative to the article’s buyer decision | Change from checkout to diagnostic or qualification when needed. |
| Can the content be reused? | Proposal, FAQ, workshop pre-work, email, or sales-call utility | Repurpose before creating unrelated new content. |

## 9. Pre-Publication Checklist

Before any asset is publicly shared, confirm that the content names the real buyer decision, aligns with a current service scope, contains no fabricated reviews or outcomes, distinguishes internal operating perspectives from universal fact, has a clear but proportionate CTA, and has an owner for reader responses. If a claim cannot be sourced, narrowed, or removed, it does not publish.
