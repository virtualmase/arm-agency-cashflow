# Keyword Cloud Primary-Source Notes

**Research date:** August 19, 2026  
**Scope:** Evidence-led keyword and content-opportunity expansion for ARM Agency’s public authority library.  
**Status:** Research record only. This document does not authorize public-page expansion or make traffic, ranking, citation, lead, or revenue claims.

## Google Search: Generative AI Features

**Source:** [Optimizing your website for generative AI features on Google Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)

Google states that its generative AI features draw on its Search index and core Search ranking and quality systems. The source identifies retrieval-augmented generation and query fan-out as mechanisms used in those experiences. It directs site owners toward foundational SEO, non-commodity people-first content, clear organization, relevant images and video when appropriate, and measurable first-party performance review.

**Keyword-cloud implications:** Retain and expand decision-centered terms around AI search foundations, technical clarity, non-commodity expert content, visual discovery, and first-party conversion learning. Do not treat query-fan-out phrases as a mandate to create a separate public page for every query variation.

**Boundary:** Google does not provide a formula for rankings, AI citations, traffic, leads, purchases, or commercial outcomes. The source explicitly warns against high-volume pages made principally to manipulate rankings or generative responses.

## Google Search: Structured Data

**Source:** [Introduction to structured data markup in Google Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data)

Google explains that structured data provides explicit clues about the meaning of visible page content. It requires page-level correspondence: markup should describe the content of the page and should not be placed on blank pages or describe information unavailable to users. Google recommends complete and accurate properties over extensive but incomplete or inaccurate markup, and identifies the Rich Results Test and post-deployment reporting as validation surfaces.

**Keyword-cloud implications:** Retain and expand decision-centered terms around structured-data governance, visible-content alignment, schema review, metadata quality assurance, and rich-result eligibility validation.

**Boundary:** The source discusses eligibility and visibility interpretation; it does not establish that a specific markup type produces a ranking, AI answer, citation, click-through rate, lead, or revenue outcome for ARM Agency or its clients.

## NIST: Generative AI Risk Management

**Source:** [Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence)

NIST describes this profile as a cross-sector companion resource to AI RMF 1.0 for generative AI. It is intended for voluntary use and addresses how organizations can incorporate trustworthiness considerations into the design, development, use, and evaluation of AI products, services, and systems.

**Keyword-cloud implications:** Retain and expand decision-centered terms around generative-AI risk management, AI lifecycle controls, AI measurement and evaluation, trustworthy AI operations, and human-owned governance decisions. These terms support ARM’s diagnostic and bounded-implementation pathways.

**Boundary:** The NIST profile is voluntary guidance. Its presence does not make ARM’s services a compliance certification or establish a client’s risk posture.

## Google Search: Image SEO and Visual Discovery

**Source:** [Google image SEO best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images)

Google’s documentation frames visual discovery as a technical and editorial system: images should be discoverable through standard HTML image elements, supported by contextual landing-page content and metadata, delivered responsively with a fallback source, and optimized for appropriate speed and quality. The guide also states that Google does not index CSS images.

**Keyword-cloud implications:** Retain and expand decision-centered terms around image indexability, image landing-page context, descriptive filenames and alternative text, responsive image delivery, image sitemaps, visual metadata, image quality, and visual-content accessibility.

**Boundary:** Google describes technical discoverability and eligibility practices, not a specific placement, traffic, conversion, or revenue outcome. Image keywords should only be used where ARM has a meaningful visual decision or audit deliverable to explain.

## Model Context Protocol: Security and Authorization

**Source:** [Model Context Protocol Security Best Practices, version 2026-07-28](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/docs/2026-07-28/tutorials/security/security_best_practices)

The MCP project’s security guidance identifies implementation-specific security risks and mitigation areas including consent, authorization scope, proxy behavior, server-side request forgery, token handling, redirect URI validation, and local-server compromise. The guidance includes requirements for per-client consent in the confused-deputy scenario described in its OAuth proxy discussion.

**Keyword-cloud implications:** Retain and expand decision-centered terms around MCP authorization, secure MCP server design, agent-tool consent, OAuth scope minimization, MCP threat modeling, tool permissions, auditability, and bounded MCP implementation.

**Boundary:** This source is technical guidance, not proof that a particular implementation is secure or compliant. ARM may describe a review or bounded build process, but must not state that a custom MCP tool eliminates risk.
