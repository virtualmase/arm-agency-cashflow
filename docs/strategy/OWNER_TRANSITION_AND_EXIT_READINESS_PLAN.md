# Owner Transition and Exit Readiness Plan

> **Draft for owner and qualified adviser review.** This is an operating-readiness plan, not a valuation, tax opinion, legal opinion, solicitation, or recommendation to sell. No transaction value, buyer suitability, tax outcome, or timeline is assumed because the supporting financial, legal, and ownership evidence is not yet assembled.

## 1. Objective

Build ARM Agency into a business that can continue operating without constant founder intervention and can credibly support future options: long-term owner operation, leadership transition, minority capital, strategic partnership, sale, or an orderly wind-down. The first objective is continuity and evidence quality; a formal transaction process should start only when the owner, advisers, and data support it.

## 2. Current readiness basis

The current platform has documented five revenue streams, account-bound checkout, a client portal, operational governance, delivery playbooks, evidence-safe marketing controls, and an owner-only decision model. These are useful foundations, but they are not evidence of a transaction-ready asset. The Entity Card identifies material gaps: legal-entity confirmation, ownership/cap-table records, formal financial statements, revenue-quality analysis, customer concentration, IP assignment, employment/contractor records, and a diligence-ready data room.

## 3. Value-driver system

| Value driver | Evidence to build | Owner decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeatable revenue | Revenue by stream, recurring versus one-time classification, contracts, renewal/cancellation behavior, and reconciled payment records | Which offers deserve investment, standardization, or retirement |
| Delivery quality and capacity | Accepted scopes, delivery playbooks, client health, handoff quality, workload/satisfaction signals, and escalation patterns | Whether growth can be accepted without degrading service or employee sustainability |
| Customer relationship quality | Account ownership, service history, referral permission, concentration analysis, and renewal rationale | How to reduce founder dependence and relationship concentration |
| Defensible operating assets | Source code, domains, design assets, documentation, processes, data rights, and IP/contractor assignment records | What must be owned, licensed, documented, or separated |
| Management depth | Role charters, delegation records, training completion, succession coverage, and decision logs | Which founder tasks can be delegated safely and which require retained authority |
| Governance and risk control | Security reviews, access records, privacy boundaries, claims review, reconciliation exceptions, and incident history | Whether the operating model is safe enough to scale or present to a counterparty |

## 4. Staged horizon

| Horizon | Primary goal | Required operating outputs | Exit-readiness test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–12 months | Establish a clean, repeatable operating baseline | Legal/entity inventory; chart of accounts; monthly close; reconciled revenue by stream; signed scopes; source-of-truth customer list; domain/IP/access inventory; agent and human role boundaries | A new operator can understand how the agency sells, delivers, supports, bills, and escalates from written records. |
| 12–24 months | Reduce founder concentration and prove repeatability | Delegated sales/service/delivery roles; standard offer margins and capacity assumptions; customer-health cadence; renewal/cancellation reasons; documented onboarding; disaster-recovery and credential-control evidence | A qualified operator can run ordinary weekly operations with the founder reviewing exceptions rather than executing every task. |
| 24–36 months | Build decision-grade performance evidence | Period-over-period financial statements; cohort/retention and concentration analysis; offer-level unit economics; evidence of delivery quality; management succession map; documented IP and customer-contract status | A qualified adviser can identify key strengths, risks, and diligence gaps without reconstructing basic facts. |
| 36–60 months | Preserve optionality for a formal transition or transaction | Adviser-reviewed quality of earnings preparation; legal and tax review; buyer/partner thesis; confidentiality plan; data-room index; management presentation; continuity and retention plan | The owner can choose an operating, succession, financing, partnership, or sale process from evidence rather than urgency. |

## 7. Founder-dependency reduction procedure

For each recurring founder task, document the decision purpose, trigger, required inputs, allowable actions, exception boundary, customer impact, systems accessed, and successor role. Train a human or governed agent on the procedure, run observed practice cases, audit outputs, and retain owner approval for material commitments. Do not delegate financial controls, security access, legal commitments, pricing exceptions, or client-scope changes merely to improve a transition narrative.

## 8. Formal process gate

Before a sale, investment, or succession process, engage appropriate legal, tax, accounting, and transaction advisers. Confirm confidentiality mode, buyer universe, ownership objectives, retained-role preferences, customer/employee communication plan, diligence protocol, data-room access rules, and decision rights. A teaser, buyer list, valuation, or financial model must be built from current sourced records; this plan intentionally does not invent those inputs.

## 9. Stop conditions

Pause a transition or transaction-preparation workstream if financial records cannot be reconciled, customer data cannot be used lawfully, the business cannot maintain delivery quality, founder dependency is hidden rather than reduced, employee sustainability is deteriorating, material security/privacy risk is unresolved, or the owner cannot state the desired outcome. Fix the operating issue before progressing the narrative.

## 10. First 90-day action register

1. Confirm the legal operating entity, owners, fiscal year end, accountant, banking/payment control owners, and authoritative document locations.
2. Establish a monthly close and revenue-reconciliation routine by revenue stream; preserve source records and explain all exceptions.
3. Create a contract/SOW, customer, renewal, cancellation, and delivery-status register with restricted access.
4. Complete an IP/domain/code/access inventory and assign named custodians plus recovery procedures.
5. Map founder tasks and select the first three candidates for documented delegation with exception review.
6. Conduct a delivery-capacity and employee-sustainability review before increasing campaign volume.
7. Create an adviser-ready data-room index; do not share customer or sensitive records until counsel and the owner define access and confidentiality controls.

## Disclosure

**Basis:** The plan uses current ARM Agency operating documentation and application context rather than historical financial statements or a transaction model. **Time:** 2026-08-19. **Assumptions:** The business is treated as private; no legal entity, fiscal year, ownership, or financial-period information is inferred. **Sources and confidence:** High confidence in the documented operating controls; low confidence in transaction readiness until primary legal, accounting, and customer evidence is assembled. **Compliance:** This is analysis and planning only, not personalized financial, legal, or tax advice.
